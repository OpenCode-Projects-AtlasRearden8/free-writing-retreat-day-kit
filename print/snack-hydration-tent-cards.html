Snack & Hydration Tent Cards

Print, cut along the dashed frame, fold on the dashed seam, and stand each card on your desk. They say things your 2 p.m. brain needs to hear.

Water first, words second.

You have been typing, not drinking. One big sip. Right now.

Coaches' note: a hydrated author writes 0% more plot holes than a dehydrated one. This is one of the few scientific facts in this kit.

Snack O'Clock

10:30 and 2:05 breaks. Get something with actual food in it — not “the last two inches of a bag.” A plate. A real snack. Then back.

Reasons this is not procrastination: 1) It's on the schedule. 2) Scheduled snack breaks are part of the retreat. 3) See item 1.

Sprint in progress

No, you cannot help me find the remote. If you are me, the answer is “keep writing.” Ends :55 / :30 / :05.

Buddy mode: point at this card instead of explaining yourself. Nod. Nod back. Perfect.

You can stop. The timer says so.

Stopping is part of the plan, not a failure of it. Stand up. Stretch. The sentence will still be here.

For afternoon-brain: “pushing through” is how two Sundays get lost. The schedule already gave you a rest. Take it.

Make your own cards

Grab card stock and a fat marker. Ideas to copy: “I already decided — the goal one-pager has the answer.” · “Sitting ≠ writing. Type one line.” · “The internet will still be insufferable after 12:15.” Keep the stack next to the snacks.
