End-of-Day Reflection

Twenty minutes at the end of the retreat. This is how one good Saturday becomes a repeatable practice instead of a nice fluke. Short answers only; then stop thinking about writing until tomorrow.

Date: ____________    Solo or with friend: ____________

The numbers (gentle numbers, not court evidence)

Words drafted: ____________ (estimate is fine)    Sprints completed: ______ of ______ planned    Longest stretch that felt good: ______ minutes

Three questions

1. What did I make today that did not exist yesterday?

2. When did today get hard, and what actually made it hard? (Be specific: “3 p.m. slump,” “the middle of chapter 4,” “my phone.”)

3. What is the one thing I want to copy into the NEXT retreat? (One change only.)

Keep / Change / Drop

KEEP (it worked)CHANGE (tweak it)DROP (retired it)

Tomorrow's first line (write it now, while it's fresh)

Last check-outs

  • Everything saved (and, if digital, backed up or committed).
  • Goal one-pager filed with this sheet — same folder, week after week.
  • Next retreat date decided: ____________ (this is the part that builds a habit)
Retreats are practice, not judged performances. Whatever today produced — whole chapters, one good scene, a messy 340 words, or mostly snacks and notes — you showed up for your writing. That's the actual skill most writers are missing, and you already have it. See you next Saturday.
