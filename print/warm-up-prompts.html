Warm-Up Prompts

Fifteen minutes, one prompt, zero judgment. Circle one with a pen. The point is warming up your writing hands, not a masterpiece.

Quick-and-easy tier

(if your brain is still on Saturday morning)

  1. Describe, in loving detail, the last snack that made you happy.
  2. Write about the piece of clothing you wear far too often.
  3. Secret talent audit: list five things you're weirdly good at.
  4. Write the text message you'd send a friend if you had no anxiety at all.
  5. Invent a useless superpower and describe a normal day using it.
Story-starter tier

(for fiction folks)

  1. The knock came again — three times, soft, absolutely certain she was home.
  2. Everyone in your family can do one small magic thing. Yours finally showed up.
  3. Your town's library has closed forever, and one shelf is yours to keep. What's on it?
  4. A cat follows you home and refuses to leave. Something about it is off.
  5. The new hire has been excellent for exactly eleven days.
Real-life tier

(for memoir, blogging, and nonfiction folks)

  1. Write about a time you were wrong in a small, funny way.
  2. What is a thing you learned recently that you wish you'd known a decade ago?
  3. Describe a room you loved that no longer exists.
  4. “The advice nobody gave me, but nobody needed to.”
  5. What did you used to be worried about that stopped mattering?
Author-brain tier

(for the book itself, applied sideways)

  1. Open your project, find the most boring scene, and write it from a different character's point of view.
  2. Summarize your work-in-progress to a confused uncle, in dialogue.
  3. Write a fake 1-star review of your own book idea. Make it kind of funny.
  4. Write your future back-cover blurb, badly, on purpose.
  5. “It was a dark and stormy night” — but actually good. Rewrite a cliché opening.

Warm-up rules

  • Write by hand if you can. It slows your brain down to warm-up speed.
  • Nobody ever reads the warm-up. Not even you. Especially not you.
  • Warm up before Sprint 1; after lunch, use one from the author-brain tier as a re-entry ramp if the afternoon feels swampy.
  • If a prompt fights you after two minutes, drop it and take another. No sunk cost.
